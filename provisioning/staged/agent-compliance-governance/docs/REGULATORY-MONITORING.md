# Regulatory Change Monitoring

## Source hierarchy

1. Enacted statutes and signed session laws from official legislative sources.
2. Adopted regulations and official agency rulemaking records.
3. Court orders and official dockets.
4. Official agency interpretations and guidance.
5. Standards-body publications and voluntary frameworks.
6. Secondary commentary only as a discovery aid, never as the sole authority for a legal status.

## Record lifecycle

For every tracked instrument:
- preserve its stable record ID;
- record the jurisdiction and instrument type;
- distinguish enacted, effective, proposed, stayed/under litigation, superseded and voluntary status;
- capture effective dates and transition periods;
- link primary sources and the source authority;
- record last verification and next review dates;
- describe applicability triggers and unresolved questions;
- route uncertain interpretation to qualified counsel;
- preserve prior status changes in version control.

## Review cadence

The initial Colorado ADMT, chatbot and litigation monitors use short review intervals because the January 1, 2027 effective date and rulemaking/court developments are near. Other records use a 30–90 day cadence. A real scheduled monitoring service is not implemented by this repository.

## Change approval

A status or obligation change must include the primary source, date, summary of change, affected controls, reviewer and next actions. Do not automatically change a control to compliant when a law is amended.
