# Legal Applicability Register — initial Colorado monitoring baseline

**Last source review:** 2026-11-02  
**Important:** This is a monitoring index, not legal advice or an applicability determination. Qualified counsel must assess the actual entity, products, data, users, processing purposes, sectors, thresholds and deployment locations.

## Colorado sources

### Colorado Privacy Act

The Colorado Attorney General states that the CPA took effect July 1, 2023. It describes threshold-based applicability, including processing personal data of at least 100,000 Colorado consumers, or at least 25,000 consumers where revenue or discounts are derived from the sale of personal data. It describes consumer rights, privacy notices, data minimization, data protection assessments, security and consent obligations.

Official sources:
- https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/
- https://coag.gov/colorado-privacy-act-rulemaking/

The Act has been amended, including SB24-041 on minors' online data and SB25-276 adding precise geolocation data to sensitive data. The Attorney General's rulemaking page describes proposed amendments. Confirm the final rule status and exact statutory text with counsel.

### SB26-189 — Automated Decision-Making Technology

The Colorado General Assembly records SB26-189 as enacted. The Attorney General states that the replacement ADMT law takes effect January 1, 2027. The official bill summary describes developer/deployer duties for covered ADMT materially influencing consequential decisions, including technical documentation, change notices, three-year records retention, consumer notices and rights to access/correct data and request meaningful human review/reconsideration.

Official sources:
- https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-189
- https://coag.gov/ai/

The Colorado Department of Law filed proposed ADMT and Chatbot Safety rules on August 11, 2026. The public comment period was stated as August 11 through October 26, 2026, subject to extension if the formal hearing continued. This register does not treat proposed rules as final law. Check the agency's latest rulemaking page and adopted text before relying on any interpretation.

### HB26-1263 — Conversational AI Service Operator Requirements

The Colorado General Assembly records HB26-1263 as enacted. The Attorney General describes requirements for public conversational AI services, including user disclosure, age estimation, minor safeguards, privacy/account tools, self-harm response protocols and annual reporting. The Attorney General states the law takes effect January 1, 2027.

Official sources:
- https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263
- https://coag.gov/ai/

Applicability depends on the statutory definition of a conversational AI service and operator. Counsel must confirm the exact operative date, exemptions, rulemaking and service scope.

### xAI LLC v. Weiser — litigation monitor

An April 27, 2026 order in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado temporarily stayed specified enforcement of SB24-205 and legislation replacing or amending it enacted during the 2026 legislative session, using a period tied to a future preliminary-injunction ruling. The order's application to SB26-189 or HB26-1263, current procedural status and duration require counsel's review. Do not treat the order as an exemption, a suspension of effective dates, or permission to ignore statutory duties.

Docket references:
- https://clearinghouse.net/case/48129/
- https://dockets.justia.com/docket/colorado/codce/1:2026cv01515/253513

Use PACER or counsel to verify the official docket and subsequent orders before any reliance.

## Voluntary guidance

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0

NIST describes the AI RMF as voluntary, rights-preserving, non-sector-specific guidance. It is not generally binding law by itself. It may still be relevant through contracts, procurement terms, internal policy or incorporation by an applicable requirement.

Official sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
- https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/

NIST indicates the AI RMF 1.0 is being revised. Monitor the NIST AI Resource Center for updates.

## Geographic scope and limitations

The owner is based in Denver, Colorado, so Colorado sources are included as an initial monitoring priority. This does not establish that any statute applies to the owner, the ecosystem, or a particular user. Denver-specific ordinances, local procurement rules, other U.S. state laws, federal sector laws and non-U.S. laws remain unassessed. Assess target markets and processing operations before expanding the legal register.

## Review policy

Every record in registry/legal-applicability.json includes status, source authority, last-verified date, next-review date, applicability summary, triggers, obligations, uncertainties and legal-review status. Changes must be source-linked and reviewed; proposed rules, litigation monitors and voluntary guidance must remain distinguishable from effective statutes.
