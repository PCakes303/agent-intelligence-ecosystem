# Privacy and AI Governance Baseline

The following are governance design topics, not legal conclusions. Apply them only after assessing the actual system and applicable requirements.

1. Maintain an inventory of AI systems, models, agents, tools, owners, purposes, data categories, users and jurisdictions.
2. Map personal data flows, controller/processor roles, sources, purposes, recipients, retention and deletion.
3. Classify use cases by impact and risk; identify consequential decisions and vulnerable user populations.
4. Define human oversight, appeal, correction, override, escalation and emergency-stop paths.
5. Provide accurate AI interaction and automated-decision notices where required.
6. Assess data minimization, sensitive data, consent, profiling, security and data protection impact requirements.
7. Maintain model/system documentation, training-data category descriptions, known limitations and material-change records.
8. Design privacy-rights request workflows, including identity checks, response timelines, appeals and deletion/correction.
9. For covered conversational AI services, assess age estimation, minor protections, self-harm response, privacy controls and annual reporting.
10. Preserve evidence with provenance, integrity checks, access controls and retention schedules.

The legal register must be reviewed before treating any item as a statutory obligation. NIST AI RMF is voluntary guidance unless another instrument makes it relevant or binding.
